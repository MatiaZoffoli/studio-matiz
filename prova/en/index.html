The starting point

What happens after delivery

Building takes weeks. Maintaining takes years, and in many contracts it is the part nobody is responsible for.

  • 01

    Frozen at the moment of delivery

    Opening hours, prices and offers change with each season, four times a year, and a website or menu that stays the same soon gives people outdated information.

  • 02

    A fee that does not say what it covers

    Fifty euros a month with no edits included comes to 600 euros a year: worth knowing exactly what that covers.

  • 03

    Visits that don't become enquiries

    Visits get counted. Enquiries, calls and bookings seldom do, and neither do the points where people leave.

How it works

Measure first, then build

A four-step process, with a clear outcome at each stage and a baseline number to start from. The path adapts to the service chosen.

  1. 01

    First meeting

    One hour to understand the work and where time is lost. Together, the number that matters is chosen (enquiries, bookings, members) and the baseline is set.

  2. 02

    Check-up

    A review of what already exists: website, profiles, email, business software, data. Within three working days of confirmation and receipt of access, a report with the map of priorities and the cost of each.

  3. 03

    Build

    Development in stages, with two rounds of revisions and a fixed price. Every stage delivers something that works.

  4. 04

    Care

    Ongoing fine-tuning: updates, adjustments and fresh measurements. Each month a report compares the numbers with the starting point.

What gets measured

The numbers that matter

Three of the goals small businesses pursue most often, and the number that measures each one.

  • Members New and returning members over time. In the case study: from 115 to 450 in one week.
  • Bookings Table or beach umbrella requests coming from website, menu and listings, compared before and after the work.
  • Enquiries Messages and quote requests received, along with the average time it takes to reply.

    About

    From hospitality to data

    Matia's photo to come

    My first memory of numbers is in my grandmother's shop. While she worked, she would keep me busy with pages of sums to work out in my head or with pen and paper. Finding the answer gave me a sense of things falling into place that I have never stopped looking for. Even today, I approach a problem believing there is a solution. When I cannot find it, I have learned to look at things differently: perhaps the question is the wrong one.

    At eleven, I was making coffee in a café in Cesenatico: drying a rack of glasses bought me ten minutes with my friends. My parents ran businesses, so work was simply part of family life. That led to twenty years in restaurants and hospitality, thirteen of them managing venues in Sydney. I learned to deal with problems as they came up, because there was no one else to pass them on to: the buck stops with you. And to read the numbers to see what works, what doesn't, and where change makes sense.

    After Covid, I decided to be more present for my family, especially my daughters. I went back to studying, first finance, then mathematics and statistics, earning two diplomas while continuing to work. I moved into data, working with public sector organisations and large companies. After twenty years of experience, I was a beginner again, but I was not starting from nothing. I brought with me the ability to read people, to understand what they mean even when they do not put it into words. That is something you do not learn in front of a screen.

    Today, I help small businesses simplify their work through data and technology, giving people more time for their families and the things they love. I have learned to read the numbers to understand a business, and to read people to understand what truly matters. Those two perspectives guide how I analyse, explore the options, choose an approach and build. With one rule: the numbers that matter are the client's. Otherwise the solution would be mine, not theirs.

    Before you ask

    Frequently
    asked
    questions

    How much does it cost?

    It depends on the job. After the first meeting a written, fixed price arrives, and it does not change along the way. The cost of the check-up is deducted from the work that follows.

    What happens after delivery?

    Ongoing care begins: updates, fixes and a first reply within one working day. Every month a report shows the hours used, the work done and the performance numbers.

    How long until delivery?

    From a few days for a menu to a few weeks for a complete website. The time is written in the quote and starts from the moment all the material arrives. Every job gets full attention, for delivery within the written time.

    Who owns the domain and the content?

    The client, always. Domain, content, data and access are registered in the client's name and, on request, managed on their behalf. At the end of the relationship everything is handed over.

    How do the ongoing care hours work?

    The monthly time devoted to requests: content changes, routine work, questions. Technical updates, security and backups do not use it up. The first month is a flat fee with unlimited hours: anything routine can be included, and what to include is decided together according to needs. At the end of the month the level is set together, looking at the data and information gathered. Major developments are quoted separately.

    Where is the work done?

    The studio is in Cesenatico. In Romagna meetings are in person, elsewhere on video, with the same method.

    Can the work be done in English?

    Yes, fully: websites, menus, wine lists, documents and support are produced in English as well as Italian.

    What data is collected?

    Only what is left by writing or booking, used to reply. The detail is in the privacy notice.
